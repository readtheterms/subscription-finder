What is a bank CSV and how do I find it?

A CSV is a plain file that lists your transactions, one per line, like a simple spreadsheet. Banks and card companies let you download one from their website.

  1. Log in to your bank or card website on a computer.
  2. Open the account and find your list of transactions.
  3. Look for "Download", "Export" or a download icon, usually near the transaction list or under account activity.
  4. Choose CSV as the file type (not PDF). Pick a date range of 6 to 12 months. Yearly charges only show up if the range is longer than a year.
  5. Come back here and choose the file you downloaded. You can add several, like a checking account and a card.

Menu names differ by bank. If you can't find it, search for "[your bank] download transactions CSV". You can open the file in a spreadsheet app to see what's inside.

Having security concerns?

Your bank details are private, so here is exactly what this page does and doesn't do.

  • Your file never leaves your computer. The page reads it with JavaScript inside your browser tab. There is no server behind it, so nothing is uploaded or saved anywhere.
  • It never asks you to log in. A CSV has no passwords, and this page never asks for your bank username, password or account number. If a page asks for those, it is not this one.
  • Nothing is kept. It stores no cookies or browser data, and your card choices are forgotten too. Close or refresh the tab and everything is gone.
  • You can check it yourself. Press F12, open the Network tab, then choose a file. You will see no upload. You can also load the page, turn off your internet, and it still works on your file.
  • The code is public. Everything is on GitHub. The only outside code is Papa Parse, a common CSV-reading library.

A CSV does list your purchases, so treat it like any private file: keep it on your own device and delete it when you're done.
